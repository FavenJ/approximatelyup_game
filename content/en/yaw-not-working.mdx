**Yaw problems in Approximately Up are RCS problems: an axis without two opposing thrusters, or knobs that were never set to yaw A and yaw B, will not rotate the ship.** The fix is in the setup, not the airframe.

## Why yaw specifically fails

The RCS system needs six maneuvering thrusters — two per axis. Each thruster has a knob assigning it to pitch A/B, yaw A/B, or roll A/B, and yaw only works when both of its jets exist and oppose each other.

The common mistake is cloning thrusters with **C** and never changing the copied knob. You end up with four jets on pitch and none on yaw, and the ship spins fine on two axes but ignores the third.

## The controller chain

RCS thrusters pair with the RCS controller wirelessly, so the wiring list is short: power to the controller from your router, and data from a 3D joystick to the controller.

If yaw dies while pitch and roll still work, the knobs are the prime suspect. If everything rotational is dead, suspect controller power or the joystick data link instead.

## Asymmetric yaw mid-flight

Depleted or failed RCS on one side of an axis causes asymmetric rotation — the ship yaws one direction but barely responds in the other. During long flights, watch the controller indicator lights: green means continue, yellow means use sparingly, and red or blinking means replace that thruster at the next garage stop.

Plan RCS battery life for the whole trip on longer flights. Running one yaw jet dry mid-coast leaves you fighting lopsided rotation for the rest of the journey.

## Quick diagnostic order

1. Press **Mouse 3** to reset stuck joysticks — rules out jammed input
2. Open garage mode with **G** and check **H** debug info for disconnected links
3. Verify two thrusters exist for yaw and their knobs read yaw A and yaw B
4. Confirm the RCS controller has its own power branch from the router

Most yaw failures are solved at step 3. Rebuilding the frame is almost never necessary.
