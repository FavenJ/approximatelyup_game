Approximately Up is a creative space simulation adventure where you build modular spacecraft, explore unknown planets, and survive chaotic missions with friends. This guide will walk you through your first ship, your first flight, and your first mission — step by step.
</GoldBox>

## Your First Ship

Approximately Up drops you on Earth with a garage, a pile of modular parts, and missions that teach building by doing. The Steam demo is fully playable now; the full game launches in August 2026 with more planets and co-op content. This guide walks through the first hour so you understand the two modes that define the game — garage edit and fly mode — before you chase the Moon or package deliveries.

## Launch the demo and learn the loop

After starting a new session, you spawn near headquarters on Earth. Open the Controls page or press M in-game for the manual. Your immediate goal is not a perfect ship; it is understanding how parts snap together, how power reaches thrusters, and how data cables link joysticks to engines.

The core loop looks like this:

- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Press <span className="font-bold text-white">G</span> to enter Garage Edit Mode — place frames, thrusters, batteries, routers, and seats.</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Wire power and data between components (see Power and Data Routing).</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Hold <span className="font-bold text-white">R</span> to enter Fly Mode — physics activate, gravity applies, and your design either flies or teaches you humility.</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Hold <span className="font-bold text-white">R</span> again to end the flight; the scene resets to pre-flight state so you can fix wiring without permanent damage.</span>

That reset is intentional. Approximately Up expects crashes. Treat each flight as a test, not a final exam.

## Garage mode essentials

In garage mode (G), you place and connect blocks freely. Useful shortcuts from day one:

- <span className="font-bold text-white text-base">G</span> — <span className="bg-gradient-to-r from-brand-theme to-brand-accent bg-clip-text text-transparent">toggle garage edit on/off</span>
- <span className="font-bold text-white text-base">Ctrl+G</span> <span className="bg-gradient-to-r from-brand-theme to-brand-accent bg-clip-text text-transparent">then drag — box-select multiple parts</span>
- <span className="font-bold text-white text-base">C</span> — <span className="bg-gradient-to-r from-brand-theme to-brand-accent bg-clip-text text-transparent">clone the selected component (garage context)</span>
- <span className="font-bold text-white text-base">X</span> — <span className="bg-gradient-to-r from-brand-theme to-brand-accent bg-clip-text text-transparent">delete selected parts</span>
- <span className="font-bold text-white text-base">H</span> — <span className="bg-gradient-to-r from-brand-theme to-brand-accent bg-clip-text text-transparent">garage debug info when something behaves oddly</span>

Build on a flat frame first. The Build Your First Ship guide expands this into a checklist: frame, battery, power router, lifting thrusters, joystick or throttle, and a seat. Skip fancy logic until you can lift off and land without exploding.

## What to build before the Moon

Before reading the Moon Landing Guide, your Earth ship should reliably:

- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Lift off with main thrusters connected through a power router</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Rotate with six RCS thrusters and an RCS controller</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Hover or descend controllably (manual levers or basic hover logic)</span>
- <span className="text-base text-gray-300">Carry a small cargo crate through a 1.2×1.2×1.2 gate — the standard package size for lost-shipment missions</span>

## Basic Controls

Hold R to launch. While flying, interact with cockpit controls by clicking levers, joysticks, and buttons, then use WASD or the mouse depending on what you selected. Press Mouse 3 (middle click) to reset a stuck lever or joystick to neutral — critical when your ship starts spinning.

Double-tap Left Shift to toggle hover dampening on foot; in ships, hover logic circuits (see Hover Logic) replace Space Engineers-style gyro dampers. For rotation in space, you need RCS thrusters — the tutorial missions introduce them after your first lift-off attempts.

## Your First Mission

Mission terminals and the objectives screen (O) list tasks by difficulty. Green missions teach single concepts: retrieve a lost package, wire a basic thruster stack, or complete a short hop. Orange missions combine systems — for example, keeping cargo powered across a longer trip.

Early rewards unlock parts in your inventory: larger frames, data routers, RCS blocks, and batteries. Completing tutorials before free-building saves time because you learn which ports accept power versus data signals.
