**Frames are the skeleton of every Approximately Up ship — all thrusters, batteries, routers, and seats mount onto them.** For a first build, a flat rectangular footprint of roughly 3×3 or 4×4 frame blocks on level ground near headquarters is the confirmed good starting point.

## Footprint and stability

A rectangular frame gives you balanced mounting points for corner thrusters and a central seat. The key failure mode is vertical: tall, narrow towers carry a high center of mass and tip over during takeoff.

Wide and flat beats tall and pretty for your first dozen builds. You can always extend the frame outward later as missions unlock larger frame parts.

## Fixing a misplaced frame

If you placed the frame crooked or too close to an obstacle, you do not need to rebuild. In garage mode, use **Ctrl+G and drag** to box-select the entire frame, then move it as one piece.

When parts refuse to connect to the frame at all, press **H** for garage debug info. Connection problems are usually wiring or alignment issues, not broken parts.

## Cargo openings

Plan the frame around what you want to carry. Standard lost-shipment mission crates measure 1.2×1.2×1.2 meters, so a gate or cargo opening slightly larger than that future-proofs the design for delivery missions.

Leave the opening accessible from outside the ship. Crawling around a landed build to wrestle a crate through a sealed frame is a common rookie frustration.

## What is not confirmed

Specific frame part names, plate variants, and their exact unlock order are **to be confirmed**. Missions are confirmed to unlock larger frames and other parts as rewards, but the detailed part list is not yet verified. This page will be updated when official part data is available.
