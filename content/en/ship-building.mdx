**Ship construction is the heart of Approximately Up: frame blocks, a battery, a power router, electric lifting thrusters, and a joystick are the minimum viable stack for leaving the pad.** Your first ship does not need to look good — it needs to lift off, respond to inputs, and survive long enough for you to learn.

## Step 1: Lay a rigid frame

Start with frame blocks on flat ground near headquarters. A simple rectangular footprint, roughly 3×3 or 4×4, gives you mounting points for thrusters and a seat. Avoid tall, narrow towers early — a high center of mass tips over during takeoff.

If you plan package missions later, add a small gate or cargo opening now. Standard lost-shipment crates fit 1.2×1.2×1.2 meters. Use **Ctrl+G box-select** to move the whole frame if you misaligned it, and press **H** for debug info when parts refuse to connect.

## Step 2: Power generation and routing

Every powered component needs a path from a battery through a power router. Place at least one battery — or a generator if you have unlocked one — and connect it to a router input, then branch outputs to your thrusters, the RCS controller, and cockpit instruments.

The rule of thumb: **thick power cables for electricity, data cables for signals**. Most first-build failures are wiring failures — thrusters plugged into data ports, or a router overloaded because nothing was branched.

## Step 3: Mount lifting thrusters

Attach electric thrusters pointing downward for an Earth takeoff. Four corners give balanced lift, and more thrusters share load and add redundancy. Connect each thruster's power port to the router.

For control, run a data cable from a 2D joystick or vertical throttle to the thruster data input. The game auto-maps familiar pairings, so a throttle-to-thruster link often works with a single data cable.

## Step 4: Cockpit and seat

Place a seat where you can reach the joystick and switches. After entering fly mode with **R**, click the throttle or joystick to focus it, then use WASD or mouse input. **Mouse 3** resets stuck controls — use it before every panic rebuild.

A kill switch on a small data branch is optional but recommended. Cutting main thrust quickly turns many bad landings into survivable ones.

## Step 5: Rotation and testing

A vertical-only template will not rotate well. Before any Moon attempt, install six RCS thrusters and one RCS controller — two opposing thrusters per axis, with each knob set to pitch, yaw, or roll A/B.

Then test: verify all power links in garage mode, hold R and listen for loose parts, throttle up slowly, and cut thrust to check a controllable descent. First flights end in crashes — that is expected. Change one variable per rebuild.
