**The velocity meter is the confirmed sensor that makes hands-free hovering possible in Approximately Up.** Combined with an accumulator, remapper, and multiplier, it forms a PD controller that holds your ship's altitude automatically — no lever babysitting required.

## What the velocity meter does

The velocity meter measures how fast your ship is moving — critically, its vertical velocity. On its own it is just an instrument; its power comes from feeding that signal into logic blocks.

For stable hover without touching the throttle, the confirmed tutorial build wires the velocity meter into a small logic circuit that adjusts thrust continuously. The ship then holds altitude by itself.

## The hover logic circuit

The confirmed component stack for the hover PD controller is four parts:

- **Velocity meter** — senses climb/sink rate
- **Accumulator** — the integral-style memory of the controller
- **Remapper** — scales the sensor signal into a usable range
- **Multiplier** — applies gain before driving the thrusters

Together they form a PD (proportional-derivative) controller: the ship corrects small altitude drifts before you even notice them.

## Tuning with the Thrust Calculator

Hover logic pairs with the in-game Thrust Calculator. The calculator compares total thrust against total mass, which tells you whether your thrusters can even hold the ship up before you tune the circuit.

Under-thrusted ships hover at full lever and still sink; over-thrusted ships bounce. Tune thrust-to-mass first, then let the logic handle the fine corrections.

## When to build it

Hover logic matters most once you fly longer missions — lunar approaches, cargo deliveries, and anywhere you need steady low-altitude flight. On the Moon, atmospheric thrusters handle the low-and-slow band, and hover logic keeps the descent calm.

For your very first ship, manual throttle is enough. Add the velocity meter circuit as your second-stage upgrade, after basic RCS rotation is working.
