**Power management in Approximately Up is about monitoring draw and respecting the RCS controller's indicator lights: green means continue, yellow means sparing use, red or blinking means replace that thruster at the next garage stop.** A dedicated battery gauge UI element is **to be confirmed** — here is what is confirmed about tracking power.

## What is confirmed about power monitoring

Every powered component draws from a battery through a power router, and total draw matters. Monitoring power draw during maneuvers is confirmed practice — starving the RCS controller mid-pitch is a documented way to lose attitude control at the worst moment.

The RCS controller exposes its own status through indicator lights. Check them during long burns and before committing to maneuvers that need full rotation authority.

## Reading the indicator lights

- **Green** — the system is healthy; continue flying normally
- **Yellow** — power is marginal; plan sparing RCS use and avoid waste
- **Red or blinking** — a thruster needs replacement at the next garage stop, or you adapt the approach using main engines only

Treat yellow as a planning signal, not an emergency. Red on a critical axis mid-flight means flying the rest of the trip with asymmetric rotation.

## Planning battery life for long flights

On longer trips — the lunar coast is the confirmed example — plan fuel and RCS battery life for the entire journey, not just the ascent. Depleted RCS on one axis mid-flight leaves you with lopsided rotation until you can garage-stop.

Give the RCS controller its own small power branch from the router. It draws little, but sharing a line with heavy lifting thrusters can cut it exactly when you need attitude control.
